# Literature Review: Autonomous AI agents for software engineering

## Introduction to Autonomous AI Agents in Software Engineering

The integration of autonomous AI agents into software engineering represents a transformative shift in the software development landscape. As outlined in recent literature, agentic software engineering (ASE) leverages autonomous agents, particularly those powered by large language models (LLMs) and artificial intelligence (AI), to enhance decision-making, code generation, and collaboration throughout the software development lifecycle. A systematic review of methodologies, frameworks, and techniques published between 2022 and 2025 highlights the substantial potential of LLM-based agents in automating various aspects of software engineering. This review identifies key trends in agent cooperation, reasoning systems, and tool integration, emphasizing the importance of iterative refinement and human-agent collaboration in the evolving software development process [Source: Methods and Techniques of Agentic Software Engineering A Systematic Literature Review.pdf].

As the role of AI in software engineering evolves, it necessitates a reevaluation of empirical research methods and practices. The emergence of AI as an active participant in the software development process—engaging in analysis, design, implementation, and research—demands that the empirical software engineering community adapt its constructs, methods, and validity criteria to effectively study AI-mediated practices. This includes addressing unique characteristics of AI-driven systems, exploring new data types, and acknowledging the challenges posed by the non-deterministic nature of AI tools. As autonomous agents become increasingly integral to development ecosystems, the need for rigorous examination of their impact on software engineering practices becomes paramount [Source: Generative AI and Empirical Software Engineering A Paradigm Shift.pdf; Source: Caging the Agents A Zero Trust Security Architecture for Autonomous AI in Healthcare.pdf].

## Applications and Case Studies of Autonomous AI Agents

The application of autonomous AI agents spans various domains, with notable advancements in programming and healthcare. In the realm of software development, systems like AlphaCode have showcased capabilities that not only approach but occasionally surpass human-level performance on specific programming challenges, highlighting the potential of autonomous agents to enhance productivity and efficiency in coding tasks [Source: Methods and Techniques of Agentic Software Engineering A Systematic Literature Review.pdf]. This transition from individual AI-assisted tools to fully autonomous multi-agent systems signifies a pivotal shift in how software engineering tasks can be approached, potentially leading to more innovative solutions and streamlined workflows.

In healthcare, the integration of autonomous AI agents raises significant security concerns, necessitating the implementation of robust frameworks such as a Zero Trust Security Architecture. This approach is crucial for safeguarding sensitive patient data and ensuring the integrity of AI systems that operate within healthcare environments [Source: Caging the Agents A Zero Trust Security Architecture for Autonomous AI in Healthcare.pdf]. Moreover, the documented vulnerabilities in agentic AI systems underscore the importance of addressing security challenges as these technologies become increasingly autonomous and pervasive. As such, the ongoing development and deployment of autonomous AI agents must be accompanied by comprehensive security measures to mitigate risks and enhance trust in their applications across various sectors [Source: Caging the Agents A Zero Trust Security Architecture for Autonomous AI in Healthcare.pdf].

## Challenges and Future Directions in Autonomous AI for Software Engineering

The integration of autonomous AI into software engineering presents a range of challenges that necessitate a reevaluation of existing constructs, methods, and validity criteria within the field. As AI transitions from being a mere tool to an active participant in software development—engaging in analysis, design, implementation, and even research—empirical software engineering must adapt to this paradigm shift. This evolution requires a focus on new research questions that reflect the unique characteristics of AI-driven systems, the adaptation of research methodologies to accommodate these changes, and an understanding of the novel data types that emerge from AI interactions. Furthermore, the non-deterministic and dynamic nature of AI tools introduces significant threats to the validity of research findings, underscoring the need for rigorous frameworks to assess the impact of these technologies on development workflows [Source: Generative AI and Empirical Software Engineering A Paradigm Shift.pdf].

As the capabilities of autonomous AI agents expand, the field of agentic software engineering is witnessing a substantial shift in ambition and functionality. Research has shown a marked increase in the autonomy levels of these agents, moving from minimal assistance under close human control to high autonomy, where agents can execute complex, multi-step tasks with minimal supervision. This progression indicates a growing reliance on AI systems that can autonomously drive software development processes, highlighting both the opportunities and challenges that lie ahead. The need for a robust security architecture, particularly in sensitive domains like healthcare, further complicates the landscape, as the integration of autonomous agents must be balanced with stringent security measures to mitigate risks associated with their deployment [Source: Caging the Agents A Zero Trust Security Architecture for Autonomous AI in Healthcare.pdf; Source: Methods and Techniques of Agentic Software Engineering A Systematic Literature Review.pdf].
