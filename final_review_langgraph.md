# Literature Review: Land subsidence monitoring in Mine sites using INSAR images

## Introduction

The monitoring of environmental changes, particularly in urban and industrial contexts, has received significant scholarly attention in recent years. A notable area of research is the assessment of mining-induced subsidence, which poses risks to both the environment and human safety. Recent studies have employed advanced remote sensing technologies, such as Radarsat-2 synthetic aperture radar (SAR) images, to monitor subsidence lakes in regions like Huainan, Eastern China. These studies demonstrate the effectiveness of SAR in detecting and analyzing the spatial evolution of subsidence areas, thereby providing critical data for environmental management and urban planning [Source: Monitoring of mining-induced subsidence lakes by Radarsat-2 SAR images in Huainan Eastern China.pdf]. Moreover, the integration of deep learning models, specifically the CBAM-enhanced VGG-UNet, has significantly improved the accuracy of detecting mining subsidence, thereby showcasing the potential of machine learning in environmental monitoring [Source: Automatic Detection for Mining Subsidence Areas Using the CBAM-Enhanced VGG-UNet Model With Long Tim.pdf].

The significance of monitoring mining-induced subsidence is intricately linked to the issue of light pollution, particularly in densely populated urban areas such as Hong Kong. Both phenomena are exacerbated by urbanization and industrial activities, which necessitate comprehensive monitoring to mitigate their impacts. A comprehensive assessment of night-sky brightness across Hong Kong has been conducted to quantify light pollution levels and their implications for human health and ecological systems. This study utilized an innovative online data reporting system that enabled participants to contribute observations, thereby enhancing the robustness of the data collected [Source: Night-sky brightness monitoring in Hong Kong - a city-wide light pollution assessment.pdf]. The findings from such assessments not only inform local policies aimed at reducing light pollution but also contribute to a broader understanding of urban environmental impacts. 

Collectively, these studies underscore the importance of utilizing advanced technologies and collaborative approaches in addressing pressing environmental challenges. They highlight the interconnectedness of mining subsidence and light pollution in urban settings, illustrating how the degradation of the environment through mining activities can exacerbate light pollution, thereby affecting both urban infrastructure and public health. This synthesis of findings emphasizes the need for integrated environmental monitoring strategies that consider the complex interactions between various urban stressors.

## Applications and Impact

The applications of advanced monitoring and detection technologies have significantly impacted environmental assessment and management. For instance, the citizen science project in Hong Kong, which focuses on monitoring night-sky brightness, exemplifies how community involvement can enhance data collection on light pollution. Participants, including secondary school students, engaged in scientific research while contributing to a comprehensive survey that utilized an online platform for data reporting. This initiative not only raised awareness about light pollution but also provided a valuable educational resource, allowing participants to submit their measurements and photographs, thereby fostering a deeper understanding of the environmental issues at hand [Source: Night-sky brightness monitoring in Hong Kong - a city-wide light pollution assessment.pdf]. 

The connection between community engagement in citizen science and the application of advanced technologies is further illustrated by examining the development of deep learning models for detecting mining subsidence areas. This technological advancement highlights the transformative potential of integrating citizen-generated data with sophisticated analytical tools. The CBAM-enhanced VGG-UNet model, for example, demonstrated improved accuracy and efficiency in identifying subsidence through the integration of real and simulated datasets. The model's performance was rigorously evaluated using standard metrics such as precision, recall, and F1-score, underscoring its capability to adapt to various mining environments [Source: Automatic Detection for Mining Subsidence Areas Using the CBAM-Enhanced VGG-UNet Model With Long Tim.pdf]. 

Together, these applications underscore the critical role of innovative methodologies in addressing contemporary environmental challenges. However, it is essential to acknowledge potential limitations and challenges associated with the integration of citizen science and advanced technologies. For instance, while citizen-generated data can enhance monitoring efforts, concerns regarding data accuracy and participant training may arise. Additionally, the reliance on advanced technologies may inadvertently marginalize communities lacking access to such tools, thereby creating disparities in environmental monitoring efforts. 

Furthermore, the implications of these technologies extend beyond immediate monitoring capabilities; they foster greater community engagement in environmental issues and can influence policy decisions. By integrating citizen science with advanced monitoring technologies, stakeholders can enhance data reliability and promote informed decision-making processes in environmental management. This synthesis of community engagement and technological innovation is crucial for developing comprehensive strategies to address environmental challenges effectively.

## Challenges and Future Directions

The integration of advanced technologies, particularly deep learning, into the monitoring of mining-induced subsidence presents both significant challenges and promising future directions. Recent studies have highlighted the rapid development of deep learning models, such as the CBAM-Enhanced VGG-UNet, which demonstrate potential in accurately detecting mining subsidence areas. However, the effectiveness of these models is often hindered by the quality of the input data, especially in regions where agricultural activities and seasonal changes introduce noise into the interferometric synthetic aperture radar (InSAR) signals. This noise can lead to fragmented timelines for surface subsidence detection, particularly during the growing season when vegetation obscures clear observations [Source: Automatic Detection for Mining Subsidence Areas Using the CBAM-Enhanced VGG-UNet Model With Long Tim.pdf]. Furthermore, the challenge of simulating realistic data scenarios—specifically, scenarios that account for varying agricultural practices and seasonal variations—remains critical, as it directly impacts the models' generalization capabilities in real-world applications [Source: Automatic Detection for Mining Subsidence Areas Using the CBAM-Enhanced VGG-UNet Model With Long Tim.pdf].

To address these challenges, a multifaceted approach is required that combines improved data collection methods with enhanced modeling techniques. For instance, the adaptation of online data reporting systems, as demonstrated in light pollution assessments, could facilitate more accurate and timely reporting of subsidence data [Source: Night-sky brightness monitoring in Hong Kong - a city-wide light pollution assessment.pdf]. This system could specifically improve the accuracy of subsidence monitoring by providing real-time updates and reducing the lag in data availability, thereby allowing for more responsive management strategies. Additionally, merging diverse datasets, such as historical land-use data with modern monitoring techniques, could yield a more comprehensive understanding of the impacts of mining activities on subsidence patterns. This integrative approach not only holds the potential to refine detection methodologies but also enhances the predictive capabilities of models. Ultimately, these advancements could lead to better management strategies for mining subsidence and its environmental implications [Source: Reconstruction of Past Human Land-use from Pollen Data and Anthropogenic Land-cover Changes Scenario.pdf]. 

In conclusion, while the integration of deep learning technologies into the monitoring of mining-induced subsidence presents significant challenges, the proposed solutions offer promising avenues for future research and application. By addressing data quality issues and enhancing modeling techniques, the field can advance towards more effective monitoring and management of subsidence phenomena.
